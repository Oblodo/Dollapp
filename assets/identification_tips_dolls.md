Where the markings actually are
--------------------------------
Most mass-market fashion dolls (Barbie and lookalikes) carry their factory
markings in four places: the right buttock, the feet, the back of the head,
and sometimes a stamp on the torso (the torso stamp is usually a mould
number, not a production year -- don't read it as a date).

Quick physical checklist
-------------------------
- Neck knob (the connector where the head meets the body): size changed
  between early years -- a smaller knob generally points earlier than a
  larger one of a similar-looking doll.
- Hands and fingers: dolls made before roughly 1968 tend to have clearly
  divided fingers; later dolls often have softer, less separated fingers.
  Dolls made in Mexico often have longer, slimmer hands with undivided
  fingers -- a manufacturing-location tell, not just an era tell.
- Feet: original ponytail-era dolls have holes in the soles for a stand's
  copper tubes. A "MADE IN JAPAN" stamp on the foot is common on early
  export dolls.
- Country-of-manufacture stamp (feet, back of head, or a fabric tag) is one
  of the best rough-era clues available without any research at all:
    Japan: roughly 1959-1972
    Mexico: roughly 1968-1990
    Hong Kong / Taiwan: around 1970 onward
    Korea: 1973 onward
  (These ranges overlap in practice -- treat them as a starting point, not a
  verdict, exactly the way Dollfind treats every AI suggestion.)
- Hair: unusual colours (platinum, light brown) or unusual factory styling
  (side parts, factory braids) usually indicate a specific, sought-after
  version rather than a common one.
- Skin tone: dolls made for the Japanese market often run pinker than the
  tanner tone used on dolls made for the American market -- useful when two
  otherwise-identical-looking dolls need telling apart.

Packaging matters as much as the doll
--------------------------------------
- NRFB (Never Removed From Box) generally commands a real premium -- often
  around 20-25% above the same doll loose -- over a played-with, removed
  example.
- NMIB / MIP (near-mint-in-box / mint-in-package) sit between loose and
  NRFB.
- A doll with its original stand, booklet, or outfit tags intact is worth
  noting explicitly in Dollfind's "condition" and "markings" fields -- those
  small details move the value more than people expect.

This is not limited to one country's market
---------------------------------------------
Vintage and collectible dolls change hands worldwide -- the "Find value &
where it's sold" panel on each item deliberately checks general
marketplaces (eBay, Etsy, Facebook Marketplace), doll-collector specialists
(Ruby Lane), European auction houses (Catawiki, LiveAuctioneers), a
sold-price research archive (WorthPoint), and clearly-labelled regional
options, rather than assuming any single country's market is the whole
picture.

How this plugs into Dollfind
------------------------------
None of the above needs a network call. It works as a static help screen
the owner can check while filling in the existing brand, era, markings and
condition fields on a record -- which then feed directly into the
marketplace search links. The better those fields are filled in from a
two-minute look at the doll itself, the better the resulting search links
are, with no AI call needed at all.
